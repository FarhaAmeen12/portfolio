Spendify — Personal Finance & Budget Management Platform

A responsive full-stack finance tracking application with category-based expense segregation, recurring transaction scheduling, balance auditing, and dynamic spending analytics charts.

The Problem

Budgeting via traditional spreadsheets is tedious and easily abandoned by undergraduates and busy professionals, while commercial banking aggregators often demand paid subscriptions and intrusive third-party banking permissions. Users need an immediate, frictionless tool that delivers clear visual clarity over cash flow without sacrificing user privacy.

The Solution

I designed Spendify with an intuitive, clean interface that provides real-time income versus expense classification, monthly category budgets, interactive spending charts, and clean data export. The platform runs with near-instantaneous load times, adapting seamlessly across desktop widescreen monitors and mobile screens down to 320px.

Key Technical Features

Real-Time Balance Ledger

Instant mathematical recalculation of net balance, monthly burn rate, and projected end-of-month runway on every transaction entry.

Dynamic Category Visualization

Custom pure SVG breakdown graphs representing spending segments by categories (Food, Housing, Transit, Education, Health) with zero external library overhead.

Monthly Category Budget Caps

Configurable financial threshold limits per category with progressive color warnings (80% amber threshold, 100% red limit).

Data Portability & CSV Export

One-click client-side CSV generator converts local transaction records into structured spreadsheets for tax filing or offline records.

Architecture & Technical Decisions

Decision 1: Zero-Dependency SVG Visualizations vs. Heavy Charting Libraries

Rather than importing heavy charting packages like Chart.js or D3 (which typically add 150KB+ minified JavaScript to the initial payload), I implemented reactive SVG pie and bar charts directly using geometric coordinate projection in vanilla JavaScript. This slashed initial page parse time to under 15ms.

Decision 2: Mobile-First Responsive Breakpoint Architecture

Designed data tables that transform fluidly from multi-column grids on desktop to stacked, swipeable card views on mobile viewports below 640px, ensuring users can record transactions comfortably with one thumb while on the move.
