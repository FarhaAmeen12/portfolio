The Problem

Connected and Autonomous Vehicles (CAVs) exchange continuous mission-critical telemetry over Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) and Controller Area Network (CAN) communication buses. Adversaries can inject spoofed sensor telemetry (falsified velocity, distance, GPS coordinates) that bypasses static threshold filters, potentially tricking autonomous brake or steering algorithms into catastrophic failure.

The Solution

During my research residency at KPR Institute of Engineering and Technology (India), I developed a modular unsupervised and semi-supervised anomaly detection pipeline in Python. By benchmarking Isolation Forests, Random Forests, and deep Autoencoders on vehicular network datasets, the framework successfully identifies malicious sensor drift in real time with sub-10ms packet evaluation windows.

Key Research Contributions

Multi-Model Benchmarking

Trained and compared three distinct mathematical architectures (Isolation Forest tree-depth scoring, supervised Random Forest classifiers, and reconstruction-loss Autoencoders) on automotive telematics.

Sub-10ms Inference Time

Optimized Scikit-learn decision paths to evaluate streaming CAN bus telemetry frames within strict deadlines required by autonomous vehicle electronic control units (ECUs).

Spoofing & Drift Discrimination

Engineered feature vectors correlating rate-of-change across accelerometer, speedometer, and wheel encoder signals to reliably identify artificial telemetry injection.

Empirical Performance Metrics

Generated ROC-AUC curves, confusion matrices, and precision-recall trade-offs to rigorously validate model confidence and minimize false positives.
